About me

From the life that "made sense" to the life that finally feels true to me.

Since I was a child, I've said I wanted "to help people." I just didn't know how.

Along the way, I did what seemed like the right thing: university, a solid career in software companies, 15 years managing projects and products. On the outside, everything looked fine. On the inside, I was slowly losing myself.

How I lost myself

When it was time to choose what to study, I had no idea what I actually wanted. I took a psychometric test. It pointed toward Economics, but I ended up in Management instead: the most "versatile" option, the one that kept the most doors open. It wasn't passion. It was logic.

Helping people got put on hold. I told myself I could always do that on the side. What mattered was having a stable job: a safe path.

For years I did what I thought was expected of me. I did well, got recognised, earned a good salary. But at some point, especially once I started working from home, I couldn't ignore it anymore: I no longer felt at home in my own life.

Getting out of bed in the morning became harder. I felt like I was slowly shrinking. The life I had built no longer felt like mine. But I still didn't know what to do about it.

The slow return to myself

That's when I started therapy with a very clear intention. I wanted to remember who I was as a child, before I'd adapted so much to what others expected of me.

Around the same time, I discovered Reiki and a spiritual path I hadn't even known existed. I started taking better care of my body, my energy and my mind.

Over time, I realised the "safe" job wasn't safe for me anymore. What was familiar wasn't true anymore either. I found the courage to quit. I didn't have a detailed plan. Just a deep sense that I needed time and space to find myself again.

It wasn't the three-month break I thought it would be. It was a life transition.

What my relationships taught me

That period of rebuilding wasn't only about work and lifestyle. It also opened my eyes to patterns I carried into my closest relationships.

This wasn't about one relationship, or even one kind of relationship. It's a thread that has run through my life, in family ties as much as romantic ones. Something in my own nature, shaped by everything I've lived, led me to give more than I received, to stretch my empathy so far into others that I'd lose my own edges, and to look for love in how much I gave rather than in who I was.

What I learned wasn't only about setting boundaries. It was understanding that what triggers me in someone else usually says more about me than about them, and that the pain someone causes often comes from what they carry themselves. Both things can be true at once: I can hold real compassion for someone's wounds, and still choose not to abandon myself for them again.

That understanding didn't arrive all at once, and it's still something I return to. But it changed how I carry myself into every relationship since, including the one I have with myself.

What changed in my life

Since then, my life stopped revolving around work and became about presence instead. About being whole, wherever I am.

I got certified in Reiki, coaching and yoga. I went deeper into my own personal and spiritual growth. I changed how I move, eat and rest. I started dancing, singing, listening to my body again.

I fell in love with a small village in Bali, where I now spend most of the year. It's where I find the quiet, the nature and the community that bring me back to myself whenever I drift.

Through the conversations I've had along the way, I realised something important: people come to me for clarity, for help turning what they feel into direction and decisions.

What I do now

Today, I support people through moments of decision and life transition.

I'm not the one with the answers. My role is to bring calm, help make sense of what feels confusing, and reflect back what's already true inside them, even before they're ready to admit it.

My work lives in that quiet space between presence, deep listening and honesty. I won't tell you what I think you should see. I'll help you see what's already yours.

What I believe in

  • That clarity doesn’t come from thinking more, but from returning to yourself.
  • That small decisions can feel as heavy as big ones.
  • That no one knows you better than you do, even when you feel lost.
  • That your truth doesn’t have to be explainable to be valid.
  • That it's easier to face doubt when you're not carrying it alone.
